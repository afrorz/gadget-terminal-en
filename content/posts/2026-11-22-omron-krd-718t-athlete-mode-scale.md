Omron Healthcare has announced the Karada Scan KRD-718T, a body composition scale with a new "athlete mode". It goes on sale in Japan on 27 November. Omron sets an open price. Kaden Watch puts the expected street price at about 20,000 yen at time of writing.

The problem Omron describes is a known one. Standard scales estimate body fat and muscle with formulas built for an average body. Omron says people with a lot of muscle tend to see body fat reading too high and skeletal muscle too low. The athlete mode uses a formula built from body data of more than 100 students in sports clubs at Nippon Sport Science University, Omron says.

## Specs

| | |

| Weight | About 2.5kg including batteries |
| Size | About 304 × 332 × 53.5mm (W × D × H) |
| Weighing range | 3 to 135kg |
| Power | Four AA alkaline batteries (test batteries included) |
| Connection | Bluetooth, with the OMRON Connect phone app |
| Display | Colour LCD on the hand grip |
| Measures | 19 items, including body fat, skeletal muscle by region (whole body, both arms, trunk, both legs), subcutaneous fat by region, body water, bone mass and lean mass |

## Why it is interesting

The scale has a hand grip on a cable. You stand on it barefoot and hold the grip. The result shows on the grip's screen. The data goes to the app, which shows muscle and fat by body part on a silhouette.

Omron says it checked the new formula against DXA, a medical scan, plus its own data from people with an average build. Professor Takashi Okada of the university, who is known on YouTube as "Bazooka Okada", helped develop it. He says that even in contest shape, ordinary scales have shown him around 20 percent body fat.

We did not find independent test results. The accuracy claims here come from Omron and its university partner.

## Can you get it outside Japan?

**We could not confirm any sale outside Japan. For now it is a Japan-market model.**

- **Where:** In Japan it is sold at electronics chains and online, plus Omron's own store, per the Omron release as summarised in press reports. We did not find an overseas listing. A Japanese proxy shopping service is the likely route once it is in stock. We did not check that any of them take it.
- **The catch:** The scale is battery-powered, so voltage and plug are not an issue. The manual and warranty are in Japanese. We could not confirm whether the OMRON Connect app shows the new silhouette view or athlete mode outside Japan. Omron runs regional app versions, such as US/CAN/EMEA.
- **Global version:** We could not confirm one. Omron says it sells scales in 81 countries, and it lists other Bluetooth body composition scales in the US and Asia-Pacific. Those are different models.
- **Safety marks:** We could not confirm any FCC or CE filing for this model. It has Bluetooth, so a radio approval would be needed for sale in other markets.
- **Alternatives you can buy locally:** Omron's BCM-500 in the US and HBF-702T in Asia-Pacific are Bluetooth body composition scales. Neither is confirmed to have an athlete mode.
