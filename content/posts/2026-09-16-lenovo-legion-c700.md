Lenovo has launched the Legion C700 in China. It is a handheld built for streaming games, not for running them on the device. It plays games from Tencent's START cloud service, and it can stream your own Windows PC over your home network with a USB dongle (Lenovo calls this LEGION STREAM).

The official price in China is 2,199 yuan. At launch on September 8 it sold for 1,799 yuan. Outside China, the import store Giztop sells it for $399 (prices at time of writing).

## Specs

| | |
| --- | --- |
| Chip | MediaTek Dimensity 7400 (4nm) |
| Screen | 7.82-inch IPS, 1920×1080, 120Hz, 500 nits |
| Battery | 8,000mAh (Lenovo says 9+ hours of cloud play or streaming) |
| Size / weight | 14.9mm thick / 556g |
| Memory | 8GB + 256GB |
| OS | Android 16 |
| Wireless | Wi-Fi 6E, Bluetooth 5.3 |
| Controls | Hall-effect + TMR sticks |
| Price | 2,199 yuan (China) / $399 (Giztop) |

## Why it is interesting

Most gaming handhelds put a powerful PC chip inside and run games locally. The C700 goes the other way. It uses a modest phone chip and a big 8,000mAh battery, and it relies on the cloud or your own PC to do the heavy work. TMR sticks are also unusual at this price.

## Can you get it outside China?

**Yes, through an importer, but not from Lenovo.**

- **Where:** Giztop ships it internationally for $399. Giztop is not an official Lenovo reseller. Check its page for customs, returns and warranty before you buy.
- **The catch:** $399 is noticeably more than the Chinese price. And Tencent START is a Chinese service. We could not confirm whether it works from outside China, so the PC streaming feature may be the main reason to buy it abroad.
- **Global version:** Lenovo has not announced one.
- **Alternatives you can buy locally:** ASUS ROG Xbox Ally and Valve Steam Deck are sold officially in many countries and run games on the device itself.
