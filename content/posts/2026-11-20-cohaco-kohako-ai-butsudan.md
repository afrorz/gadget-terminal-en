A butsudan is a small household altar used in Japan to remember the dead. Alfa Club Musashino, a Japanese company, will start selling an AI version called Kohako on November 24. The unit is a compact box that plays back family photos and videos, instead of only holding a portrait and a memorial tablet.

The press release was issued on November 17. Kaden Watch, a Japanese appliance site, also covered the launch.

## Specs

| | |
| --- | --- |
| Size | 180 × 180 × 251mm (349mm tall with the lift unit pulled out) |
| Weight | About 4.5kg, varies by unit because it uses natural wood |
| Power | AC adapter with a USB Type-C connector, included |
| Connectivity | Wi-Fi, 2.4GHz and 5GHz |
| Sensors | Motion sensor, touch sensor on the top, microphone |
| File formats | JPEG, PNG, HEIC, GIF, WebP, MP4, MOV |
| Memorial tablets | Up to four, sold separately |
| Price | 495,000 yen including tax, at time of writing |

The press release does not list screen specifications. It lists storage as "64" with an unclear unit, so we do not give a figure.

## Why it is interesting

You upload photos and videos from a phone to a website. They sync to the unit over Wi-Fi. The company says AI tags and sorts them, and shows some more often than others. A smiling photo, for example, appears more often than a landscape, according to its description.

When someone stands in front of the unit, the motion sensor starts the display. Touching the top plays the sound of an "orin," a small bowl bell used in Buddhist practice. There is also a "time message" feature, where a person can leave a video or message to be delivered on a chosen date, such as a grandchild's school entrance.

Memorial tablets can be added for 46,200 yen each including tax, and that price covers the tablet, engraving and shipping. The unit also has a storage space for keepsakes.

The price rose from earlier plans. Reports from 2025 said about 350,000 yen. The launch price is higher. We found no explanation from the company.

## Can you get it outside Japan?

**We could not confirm any way to buy it outside Japan.**

- **Where:** The release names the official Kohako site, cohaco.me, as the sales channel. It does not say whether it ships abroad. We could not confirm overseas delivery.
- **The catch:** The setup depends on a Japanese-language website and phone workflow, according to the release, which is in Japanese. We could not confirm that the unit or its site has an English mode. The photo sync needs the company's web service, so it would stop working if that service ended. The memorial tablet service is made for Japanese Buddhist customs. We could not confirm electrical details beyond a USB-C power adapter. We found no FCC or CE listing.
- **Global version:** We found none, and no announcement of one.
- **Alternatives you can buy locally:** A Wi-Fi digital photo frame does the photo part in most countries. It will not do the altar part. We did not test any, so we name none here.
