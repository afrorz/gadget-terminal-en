Hitachi Global Life Solutions has announced the Rakumente Stick PV-BG300R, a cordless stick vacuum that comes with an automatic dust-collection dock. It goes on sale in Japan in early December. Hitachi sets no list price. Kaden Watch puts the expected street price at about 71,000 yen at time of writing.

The idea is simple. You put the vacuum back on its dock, and the dock pulls the dust out of the vacuum's dust case. You then empty the dock about once every two months, according to Hitachi, depending on the dirt, the amount and how you use it.

Hitachi's own page lists the weight, the dock and the color. The runtime, dimensions and noise figures come from Kaden Watch only. Its battery-size line was garbled, so we leave it out.

## Why it is interesting

Most self-emptying vacuum docks add bulk and weight. Hitachi's pitch is the opposite: a light body that still has a dock. Hitachi calls the weight "light" and the body 1.1kg by its own measure.

Two features stand out in the Hitachi and Kaden Watch descriptions:

- **Dust button.** A "Gomi Dash" button on the dock pushes the compressed dust out as a single lump. Kaden Watch adds that putting tissue in the dock reduces filter clogging.
- **Filter care.** During collection, airflow knocks fine dust off the main filter. Hitachi says this is meant to keep suction steady and cut upkeep.

Kaden Watch also lists a light that shows dust, a loop-tipped brush that resists hair tangling, a flap behind the head and a self-propelled assist. It says the exhaust keeps about 99% of fine dust. We have only the maker's claims for all of these.

Hitachi also notes that more than 10% of the plastic by mass is recycled, the exterior resin is unpainted, and the lithium-ion battery is a cassette type that is easy to remove for disposal.

## Can you get it outside Japan?

**We could not confirm any way to buy it outside Japan. Treat it as Japan-only for now.**

- **Where:** Hitachi's page points to its Japanese online store. We found no overseas seller for this model. Hitachi stick vacuums are sold in some other markets, such as Singapore, Malaysia, Australia and the Gulf, but the listings we found were for other models, and none was a self-emptying dock model.
- **The catch:** Hitachi's page and the support material are in Japanese. Japan runs on 100V with type A plugs. We could not confirm whether the dock and charger work at 220 to 240V. Check the rating label before you use one on a higher-voltage supply. We also could not confirm whether the warranty applies outside Japan.
- **Global version:** We found no announcement of one. We found no FCC or CE listing.
- **Alternatives you can buy locally:** Several makers sell stick vacuums with self-emptying docks in Europe and North America. We did not compare them with this model, so we do not name one here.
